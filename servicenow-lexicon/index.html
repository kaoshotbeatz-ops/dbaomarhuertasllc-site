    Foundations

    FOUNDATIONSServiceNow, in One Sentence1
    WHAT IT IS

    ServiceNow is a single cloud platform for running the work of a whole organization.

    Most companies juggle dozens of disconnected systems. ServiceNow takes that scattered work — requests, approvals, tasks, records — and puts it on one shared foundation where everything lives together and talks to itself.

    The Core Idea

    Instead of buying separate software for IT, HR, customer service, and operations, you build all of it on the same platform. One login, one data model, one way of working.

    ONE PLATFORM
    Many business functions run on the same shared system rather than separate tools.
    WORKFLOW ENGINE
    At its heart it moves work from person to person automatically until it is done.

    Key: Think of ServiceNow not as one app, but as the ground everything else is built on.

    THE PROBLEMThe Mess It Cleans Up2
    WHY IT EXISTS

    Before a platform like this, work hides inside email threads, spreadsheets, and a dozen tools that do not talk to each other.

    Tool Sprawl

    Each team buys its own software. A request that crosses two teams falls through the cracks because the systems never share information. Nobody can see the whole picture.

    • Email chaos. Requests live in inboxes, so nothing is tracked, measured, or guaranteed to get done.
    • Spreadsheet sprawl. Critical lists get copied, edited, and emailed until no one knows which version is true.
    • No accountability. Without a shared record, it is impossible to say who owns what or how long it took.

    Key: The real problem is not too few tools — it is too many disconnected ones.

    ServiceNow replaces the scattered patchwork with one trustworthy place where every request becomes a tracked, visible record.

    THE NOW PLATFORMThe Now Platform3
    THE FOUNDATION

    The Now Platform is the shared engine underneath every ServiceNow product.

    Because everything sits on one platform, a customer complaint, an IT outage, and an HR onboarding task can all reference the same people, the same equipment, and the same history.

    One Data Model

    A single, shared structure stores all the information. When one team updates a record, everyone who needs it sees the change instantly. There is no copying between systems.

    ONE PLACE TO WORK
    People do their daily tasks inside the same environment, no matter which department they are in.
    SHARED SERVICES
    Automation, reporting, and security are built once and reused everywhere.

    Key: The power comes from connection — everything referencing the same single source of truth.

    INSTANCESYour Instance & Environments4
    WHERE IT LIVES

    Each customer gets their own private copy of ServiceNow, called an instance.

    An instance is a dedicated, walled-off version of the platform with your data and your customizations. Most organizations run several instances so they can build and test safely before changes reach real users.

    The Typical Lineup

    EnvironmentPurpose
    DevelopmentWhere builders create and change things.
    TestWhere changes are verified before release.
    ProductionThe live system real users depend on.

    Development and test are called sub-production instances. To keep test realistic, teams make a clone — a fresh copy of production data — so testing happens against real-world conditions.

    Key: Build in dev, prove it in test, release to production — never experiment on the live system.

    RELEASESReleases & Upgrades5
    STAYING CURRENT

    ServiceNow ships major upgrades on a predictable schedule, each with a city name.

    Twice a year a new family release arrives — named alphabetically after cities (for example, Washington, Xanadu, Yokohama). Each one adds features and improvements that every customer eventually adopts.

    How Support Works

    ServiceNow supports the current release and the one before it — a policy called N-1. This gives customers a window to test and upgrade on their own timeline without falling out of support.

    2x
    major releases per year
    N-1
    supported versions
    A–Z
    city naming order

    Key: Upgrades are expected and routine — staying within N-1 keeps you supported and secure.

    DATA MODELTables, Records & Fields6
    HOW DATA IS STORED

    Everything in ServiceNow is stored as records in tables — like a giant set of smart spreadsheets.

    The Three Building Blocks

    • Table. A collection of similar things, such as all incidents or all employees.
    • Record. One single item in a table — one incident, one person, one laptop.
    • Field. A single piece of information on a record, such as its status or who it is assigned to.

    Tables can build on one another. A specialized table can inherit everything from a general one and add its own details — so common features are defined once and reused.

    RELATED RECORDS
    A field can point to another record, linking a request to the person who made it.
    ONE TRUTH
    Update a record once and every place that references it reflects the change.

    Key: Tables hold records, records hold fields — that simple pattern underlies the entire platform.

    THE INTERFACEForms, Lists & the UI7
    USING IT

    People mostly interact with ServiceNow through two simple views: lists and forms.

    Lists and Forms

    A list shows many records at once, like rows in a table — handy for scanning, sorting, and filtering. A form shows one record in detail, with all its fields laid out for reading and editing.

    LIST VIEW
    See and filter many records. Click any row to open it.
    FORM VIEW
    Work on a single record — fill in fields, add notes, save.

    Forms guide users with required fields, helpful defaults, and sections that group related information. Most people never need to think about the tables underneath — they just open a form and do their work.

    Key: Lists are for finding; forms are for doing. Together they cover almost all daily use.

    NAVIGATIONNavigator & Workspaces8
    FINDING YOUR WAY

    ServiceNow offers different front doors depending on the kind of work you do.

    Two Ways In

    The classic Navigator is a searchable menu on the left that opens any list or page — flexible and powerful for builders and power users.

    Workspaces are modern, focused screens built for a specific job. They put everything an agent needs — lists, details, and tools — on one streamlined page.

    NAVIGATOR
    The all-purpose menu to reach any part of the platform quickly.
    SERVICE OPERATIONS WORKSPACE
    A unified screen for IT operations teams to triage, investigate, and resolve issues in one view.

    Key: Navigator gives you everything; workspaces give you exactly what one role needs.

    ACCESS CONTROLRoles, Groups & Access9
    WHO CAN DO WHAT

    ServiceNow carefully controls who can see and change each piece of information.

    The Pieces

    • Role. A label that grants a set of permissions, such as the ability to manage incidents.
    • Group. A collection of people, often a team, that can be assigned roles all at once.
    • ACL. An access control rule that decides, record by record and field by field, who may read or write.

    Access Control Lists (ACLs) are the gatekeepers. Before showing or saving anything, the platform checks the relevant ACL to confirm the user is allowed.

    Key: Give people roles through groups, and let ACLs enforce the fine-grained rules automatically.

    This layered approach means sensitive data stays protected without anyone having to police it by hand.

    AUTOMATIONThe Automation Primitives10
    MAKING IT SMART

    Small, focused rules make ServiceNow react intelligently as people work.

    Three Workhorses

    BUSINESS RULES
    Run on the server when records are saved — to validate, calculate, or trigger follow-on actions.
    CLIENT SCRIPTS
    Run in the browser as a user fills out a form, reacting instantly to what they type.
    UI POLICIES
    Show, hide, or require fields on a form based on conditions — no coding needed.
    WHY IT MATTERS
    Together they keep data clean and guide users without anyone watching over their shoulder.

    Key: Business rules act behind the scenes; client scripts and UI policies shape the form in front of you.

    LIFECYCLENotifications, SLAs & States11
    WORK IN MOTION

    Every record moves through a lifecycle, and ServiceNow keeps that journey on track.

    How Work Progresses

    Records carry a state — such as New, In Progress, or Closed — that shows where they are. Flows and rules move work to the right person at each step.

    • Notifications. Automatic emails or alerts tell people when something needs their attention.
    • SLAs. Service Level Agreements set time targets — like resolving an issue within four hours — and track whether they are met.
    States
    where work stands
    SLAs
    time commitments
    Alerts
    who to nudge

    Key: States show progress, SLAs hold it to a clock, and notifications keep everyone moving.

    PACKAGINGScoped Apps & the Store12
    HOW IT'S BUNDLED

    Functionality is packaged into self-contained applications that can be installed cleanly.

    Scoped Applications

    A scoped app is a bundle of tables, rules, and screens that lives in its own namespace. Because it is walled off, it cannot accidentally interfere with other apps on the same instance — making it safe to install, update, or remove.

    THE STORE
    A marketplace of ready-made apps from ServiceNow and partners that install onto your instance.
    BUILD YOUR OWN
    Organizations create custom scoped apps for needs unique to their business.

    This packaging model is why the platform scales: capabilities arrive as tidy units instead of tangled custom code.

    Key: Scoped apps keep features isolated and safe; the Store makes proven ones a click away.

    CMDB & ITOM

    CMDBThe CMDB13
    SYSTEM OF RECORD

    The Configuration Management Database is the single, trusted inventory of everything an organization owns and runs in IT — the master list that everything else trusts.

    Think of it as the company's IT census. Every server, laptop, application, database, network switch, and cloud resource is recorded once, in one place, with its key details kept current. When teams need to know what they have, who owns it, and how it connects, they look here.

    Key: Without a CMDB, IT runs on spreadsheets and tribal knowledge. With one, every other ServiceNow capability — incidents, change, security, cost — gets accurate, shared facts.

    Why it is the foundation

    • Shared truth. Support, security, and operations all read the same data.
    • Impact awareness. Before a change, you can see what it might break.
    • Accountability. Every item has an owner and a lifecycle.
    CIsConfiguration Items & Classes14
    BUILDING BLOCKS

    A Configuration Item, or CI, is a single thing the CMDB tracks — one server, one app, one database. CI classes group similar items so each kind stores the right details.

    Every record in the CMDB is a CI. A class is the template that decides which fields a CI carries: a server CI tracks CPU and memory; a software CI tracks version and publisher. Classes are arranged in a family tree, from broad to specific.

    CI
    One tracked thing, with an owner, status, and identifying details.
    CLASS
    The category that defines what fields a CI of that type holds.

    From general to specific

    • Hardware. A broad parent class for physical gear.
    • Server. A child of hardware, with server-specific fields.
    • Linux Server. A more specific child still, inheriting all the above.

    Key: Right class, right fields, no duplicates — that is a healthy CI.

    RELATIONSHIPSRelationships & Dependency Views15
    CONNECTIONS

    CIs rarely stand alone. Relationships record how items depend on one another — an app runs on a server, which uses a database, which sits on storage.

    A list of parts is useful, but the real value is knowing how the parts connect. Relationships turn a flat inventory into a living map. A dependency view draws that map visually, so anyone can trace a chain from a customer-facing service down to the hardware beneath it.

    Why connections matter

    • Impact analysis. Patching one server? See every app and service that rides on it first.
    • Faster root cause. When a service breaks, follow the relationships down to the failing part.
    • Smarter change. Plan maintenance windows around what is actually affected.

    Key: An item without relationships is an orphan — data with no story. Relationships are what make a CMDB worth having.

    CSDMCSDM Overview16
    THE BLUEPRINT

    The Common Service Data Model is ServiceNow's recommended standard for how to organize your CMDB — an agreed-upon blueprint so everyone models data the same way.

    Left to their own devices, teams name and structure things differently, and the data becomes a mess. CSDM is a shared rulebook: it says which classes to use, how services should be defined, and how everything links together. Follow it, and your CMDB stays consistent, reportable, and ready for advanced features.

    Key: CSDM is not a product you install — it is a framework, a set of best practices for arranging the data you already collect.

    What adopting it gives you

    • Consistency. Everyone speaks the same data language.
    • Service focus. Technology is tied to the business services it supports.
    • Future-ready. Cost, risk, and AI features expect CSDM-shaped data.
    CSDM DOMAINSCSDM's Four Domains17
    FOUR PARTS

    CSDM organizes data into four domains that move from raw building blocks to the services customers actually buy and use.

    FOUNDATION
    The basics everything else needs: locations, companies, people, and groups.
    DESIGN
    How services are planned and defined before they go live — the service portfolio.
    MANAGE TECHNICAL
    The real running technology: servers, apps, and the technical services IT operates.
    SELL / CONSUME
    The business and customer-facing services people actually request and pay for.

    Key: Read the domains left to right — foundation supports design, design becomes managed technology, which is sold and consumed as services.

    CMDB HEALTHCMDB Health18
    DATA QUALITY

    A CMDB is only as useful as it is trustworthy. CMDB Health measures data quality across three dimensions so you know whether to rely on what it says.

    1
    Correctness
    2
    Completeness
    3
    Compliance
    • Correctness. Is the data accurate and free of duplicate records for the same thing?
    • Completeness. Are the required fields actually filled in, not left blank?
    • Compliance. Does the data follow the standards, like CSDM, the organization set?

    Key: Bad data is worse than no data — it leads teams to wrong decisions with confidence. Health dashboards score these dimensions so owners can fix the weakest spots first.

    DISCOVERYDiscovery19
    AUTO-INVENTORY

    Discovery is how ServiceNow finds out what is actually on the network and records it — automatically — so the CMDB reflects reality instead of guesswork.

    Rather than asking people to enter every device by hand, Discovery scans the network, identifies each device, logs in safely to read its details, and writes accurate CIs into the CMDB. It runs on a schedule, so the inventory stays current as things change.

    How it works, in plain steps

    • Scan. Look across a range of network addresses for live devices.
    • Classify. Figure out what each one is — a Windows server, a switch, a database.
    • Probe. Gather details: software installed, configuration, running services.
    • Record. Create or update the matching CI, avoiding duplicates.

    Key: Discovery is agentless — it does not require software installed on every machine. It reaches out securely through a helper called the MID Server.

    MID SERVERThe MID Server20
    SECURE BRIDGE

    The MID Server is a small piece of software that runs inside your own network and acts as a secure bridge between ServiceNow's cloud and your internal systems.

    ServiceNow lives in the cloud, but your servers and devices live behind your firewall. The MID Server — Management, Instrumentation, and Discovery — sits in the middle. ServiceNow hands it work to do, and it carries that work out locally, then sends results back.

    Why it matters

    • Safe by design. The MID Server reaches out to ServiceNow; you never have to open your firewall inward.
    • Local muscle. It does the scanning, probing, and integrating from inside the network where the devices are.
    • Agentless enabler. It lets Discovery work without installing software on every machine.

    Key: No MID Server, no Discovery, Service Mapping, or most integrations — it is the trusted hands inside your network.

    SERVICE MAPPINGService Mapping21
    THE SERVICE MAP

    Service Mapping builds a top-down picture of how a business service is delivered — tracing it from the front door all the way down to every server and database that powers it.

    Discovery finds the parts; Service Mapping shows how those parts come together to deliver something people use, like an online checkout or an employee portal. It starts at an entry point, follows the actual traffic, and draws the complete map of supporting CIs and their relationships.

    What you gain

    • Service-level visibility. See the whole service, not just scattered components.
    • Precise impact. Know exactly which service a failing component affects.
    • Living maps. The map updates as the environment changes.

    Key: Discovery answers what do we have; Service Mapping answers how does this service actually work and what holds it up.

    EVENT MGMTEvent Management22
    SIGNAL FROM NOISE

    Monitoring tools generate a flood of alerts. Event Management filters, groups, and prioritizes that noise into a short list of alerts worth acting on.

    A large environment can throw off thousands of raw events an hour — most of them duplicates, blips, or symptoms of one underlying problem. Event Management collects events from many tools, removes the noise, and bundles related ones into a single meaningful alert tied to the affected service.

    From flood to focus

    • Collect. Pull events in from monitoring tools across the environment.
    • Deduplicate. Collapse repeats so one issue is one alert.
    • Correlate. Group related events that point to the same root cause.
    • Act. Raise a prioritized alert — and open an incident automatically if needed.

    Key: The goal is fewer, smarter alerts — so teams chase real problems, not noise.

    AIOPSHealth Log Analytics & AIOps23
    INTELLIGENT OPS

    AIOps applies machine learning to operations data, spotting trouble before it becomes an outage. Health Log Analytics reads system logs to learn what normal looks like — and flags what is not.

    Traditional monitoring waits for something to cross a fixed threshold. AIOps is smarter: it studies patterns over time, learns each system's normal rhythm, and surfaces unusual behavior early — often before users notice anything.

    What it brings

    • Anomaly detection. Learns normal, then flags meaningful deviations automatically.
    • Log insight. Health Log Analytics scans huge volumes of logs to find early warning signs.
    • Root-cause hints. Connects related signals to point at the likely cause faster.

    Key: AIOps shifts operations from reacting after the fact to predicting and preventing — using the CMDB and service maps to understand impact.

    CLOUD VISIBILITYCloud & Container Visibility24
    MULTI-CLOUD

    Modern IT runs across several cloud providers and fast-changing containers. ServiceNow extends Discovery to see all of it and keep the CMDB current with the Service Graph Connector.

    Cloud resources spin up and disappear in minutes, and containers can come and go in seconds — far too fast for manual tracking. ServiceNow connects directly to cloud platforms to inventory what is running, and uses Service Graph Connectors to pull trusted data from outside tools straight into the CMDB.

    MULTI-CLOUD
    One inventory spanning multiple public cloud providers, no silos.
    CONTAINERS
    Visibility into short-lived, fast-moving container workloads.

    Key: The Service Graph is the modern, governed pipeline for getting accurate data into the CMDB — so even ephemeral cloud resources show up where decisions get made.

    ITSM

    ITSMIT Service Management25
    ITSM CLUSTER

    ITSM is the discipline of running IT like a service business — with front doors, queues, and promises — instead of a pile of cables and ad-hoc favors. It is where ServiceNow began.

    Imagine a company where every laptop problem, password reset, and new-hire setup goes through one organized front desk. That front desk takes the request, routes it to the right person, tracks it to completion, and keeps a record. That is ITSM.

    Key: ITSM treats IT support as a set of repeatable services with clear ownership, timelines, and quality measures — not random firefighting.

    • Why here. ServiceNow was founded to fix messy IT help desks, so ITSM is its original and strongest home.
    • Built on ITIL. Most ITSM follows ITIL, a widely used playbook of best practices.
    • One platform. Incidents, requests, changes, and knowledge all live in one connected system.
    INCIDENTIncident Management26
    ITSM CLUSTER

    An incident is anything that breaks or slows a service users depend on. The single goal of incident management is to restore normal service as fast as possible — even with a temporary fix.

    If email is down, the job is not to find why it broke. The job is to get email working again. Investigating the deeper cause comes later, and it has its own process.

    RESTORE
    Get the user working again quickly, by any safe means.
    TRACK
    Log it, assign a priority, and keep the user informed.
    • Priority. Set by combining impact (how many affected) and urgency (how time-sensitive).
    • Workaround. A temporary fix that restores service while the real cause waits.

    Key: Speed of restoration is the measure of success — not elegance of the fix.

    MAJOR INCIDENTMajor Incidents & Swarming27
    ITSM CLUSTER

    A major incident is a high-impact outage — a store-wide checkout failure, a hospital records system down. It needs a faster, bigger, more coordinated response than an everyday ticket.

    Instead of passing a ticket from team to team, the organization swarms: it pulls the right experts into one virtual room at the same time to solve it together, in real time.

    • Major incident manager. A single coordinator who runs the response and shields the experts from chaos.
    • Swarming. Network, app, and database specialists collaborate live instead of in a relay race.
    • War room. A bridge or chat channel where status, decisions, and updates are centralized.
    • Communication. Frequent, plain updates go to leadership and affected users.

    Key: Major incidents trade the orderly queue for all-hands speed, with one person owning the outcome.

    PROBLEMProblem Management28
    ITSM CLUSTER

    If incidents are fires, problem management is fire prevention. Its job is to find the underlying cause behind recurring incidents so they stop happening.

    When the same outage returns every Monday, incident management keeps putting it out. Problem management asks why — and fixes the root so the fire never restarts.

    ROOT CAUSE
    The true underlying reason incidents keep occurring.
    KNOWN ERROR
    A documented cause with a proven workaround on file.
    • Reactive. Investigate after repeated incidents reveal a pattern.
    • Proactive. Spot weak points and fix them before they cause outages.
    • Known error database. A reference so support resolves repeat issues instantly.

    Key: Incident management restores service; problem management removes the cause for good.

    CHANGEChange Management29
    ITSM CLUSTER

    Most outages are caused by changes — an upgrade, a new server, a config tweak. Change management makes change safe by reviewing, approving, and scheduling it before it happens.

    It is the seatbelt for IT: it does not stop you from moving, it stops a small move from becoming a crash.

    TypeWhat it is
    StandardLow-risk, pre-approved, routine (e.g. a known password reset job)
    NormalNeeds assessment and approval before scheduling
    EmergencyUrgent fix for an outage; fast-tracked approval
    • Risk assessment. Weigh likelihood and impact before saying yes.
    • Rollback plan. A documented way to undo the change if it fails.

    Key: Change management balances progress against risk — move forward, but with a safety net.

    CABThe CAB & Change Calendar30
    ITSM CLUSTER

    The Change Advisory Board (CAB) is the group that reviews and approves higher-risk changes. The change calendar shows everyone what is scheduled and when.

    Think of the CAB as a flight control tower: it makes sure two risky changes do not take off at once and collide.

    • CAB members. Representatives from IT, business, and security who assess risk together.
    • Change calendar. A shared schedule that prevents conflicts and surprises.
    • Blackout window. A protected period (like holiday sales) when no risky changes are allowed.
    • Maintenance window. A planned time set aside for safe, expected changes.

    Key: The CAB approves; the calendar coordinates — together they keep changes from colliding.

    REQUESTRequest Management & the Catalog31
    ITSM CLUSTER

    A request is asking for something you are entitled to — a new laptop, software access, a phone. The Service Catalog is the menu of those somethings, like an online store for the workplace.

    Unlike an incident, nothing is broken. The user simply wants something delivered, and the catalog turns that into an orderly, trackable order.

    CATALOG ITEM
    A single orderable thing, with a form and clear cost.
    FULFILLMENT
    The behind-the-scenes steps that deliver the order.
    • Request vs. incident. Request = I want something; incident = something is broken.
    • Workflow. Approvals and tasks fire automatically once you submit.

    Key: The catalog turns vague emails into clean, approved, trackable orders.

    KNOWLEDGEKnowledge Management32
    ITSM CLUSTER

    Knowledge management captures what the team learns — how-tos, fixes, answers — so it can be reused instead of rediscovered. The dream: users help themselves.

    When a good article exists, a user finds the answer and never files a ticket. That avoided ticket is called deflection, and it saves everyone time.

    • Knowledge article. A clear, searchable write-up of a solution or process.
    • KCS. Knowledge-Centered Service — capture knowledge as you solve tickets, not afterward.
    • Self-service deflection. Users solving their own issues via articles, reducing ticket volume.
    • Feedback loop. Readers flag stale or unclear articles so they stay accurate.

    Key: The cheapest, fastest ticket is the one a good article prevented from ever being filed.

    SLMService Level Management33
    ITSM CLUSTER

    Service level management is about promises and proof. It sets clear targets for how fast and how well services are delivered — then measures whether those targets are met.

    A Service Level Agreement (SLA) is a promise to the customer: resolve high-priority issues within four hours, for example. The system tracks the clock automatically.

    TermPromise between
    SLAIT and the customer or business
    OLAInternal teams supporting that SLA
    Underpinning contractIT and an outside vendor
    • Breach. When a target is missed — tracked and reported.
    • Chained promises. An SLA only holds if the OLAs and contracts beneath it do.

    Key: SLAs are public promises; OLAs and underpinning contracts are the internal promises that keep them.

App Engine gives you the building blocks — data tables, forms, workflows, and security — so a team can assemble a real working app on the platform instead of buying or coding one from scratch.

    LOW-CODE
    Drag, configure, and point-and-click your way to a working app — less hand-written code.
    GOVERNED
    Apps inherit the platform’s security, audit trail, and lifecycle controls automatically.

    Key: App Engine turns “we need a system for that” into something a small team can deliver in weeks, not months.

    Building on the platform

    STUDIOApp Engine Studio & UI Builder74
    BUILD & AUTOMATION

    These are the visual workspaces where builders actually create an app — the design tools, not the engine underneath.

    App Engine Studio is a guided, step-by-step canvas. You define your data, add the experiences users see, layer in logic, and set who can access what — all from one place, with helpful prompts along the way.

    UI Builder is the page designer. It lets you arrange the screens — lists, forms, charts, buttons — by dragging components onto a page and configuring how they behave, without writing front-end code.

    • App Engine Studio. The “build the whole app” cockpit.
    • UI Builder. The “design the screens” tool inside it.
    • Components. Reusable visual pieces you snap together.

    Key: Studio assembles the app; UI Builder makes it look and feel right for the people using it.

    FLOW DESIGNERFlow Designer: Automation Without Code75
    BUILD & AUTOMATION

    Flow Designer lets you automate routine work by describing it in plain steps — “when this happens, do that” — instead of writing scripts.

    A flow is a recipe. It starts with a trigger (a record is created, a date arrives, a form is submitted) and then runs a sequence of actions (send an email, create a task, ask for approval, update a field). You build it by stacking readable steps in a visual editor.

    TRIGGER
    The event that starts the flow — the “when.”
    ACTIONS
    The steps it performs in order — the “then.”

    Because flows are visual, the people who understand the process — not just engineers — can read, review, and adjust them.

    Key: Flow Designer replaces manual, repetitive busywork with reliable, repeatable automation anyone can follow.

    ACTIONSActions, Subflows & the Flow Ecosystem76
    BUILD & AUTOMATION

    If a flow is a recipe, actions are the individual steps — and subflows are mini-recipes you can reuse across many flows.

    • Action. A single reusable step, like “create a record” or “post a message.” Ships ready-made or built custom.
    • Subflow. A small flow packaged so other flows can call it — build once, reuse everywhere.
    • Action Designer. The tool for making your own actions when no ready-made one fits.

    This matters because it keeps automation tidy. Instead of copying the same five steps into ten flows, you build a subflow once and call it. Fix it in one place and every flow improves.

    Key: Reusable actions and subflows make automation faster to build, easier to maintain, and consistent across the business.

    INTEGRATIONHUBIntegrationHub & Spokes77
    BUILD & AUTOMATION

    IntegrationHub lets your flows reach out and talk to other systems — email tools, chat apps, cloud services — without custom plumbing.

    Most work crosses system boundaries: a request here might need to create a ticket there, message a team, or update a record in another tool. IntegrationHub provides pre-built connectors so a flow can do that as just another step.

    SPOKE
    A ready-made connector for a specific outside system — the bundle of actions that talk to it.
    ACTION
    One thing the spoke can do in that system, like “send a message” or “create an item.”

    You drop a spoke’s action into a flow, fill in a few details, and the connection just works — no need to learn each system’s technical details.

    Key: Spokes turn complex system-to-system connections into simple, reusable flow steps.

    WEB SERVICESREST, SOAP & Web Services in Plain English78
    BUILD & AUTOMATION

    Web services are how two software systems talk to each other over the internet — one asks, the other answers.

    Think of it like ordering at a counter. Your system sends a request (“give me this customer’s record”), the other system sends back a response (the data, or a confirmation). The rules for how they phrase those messages are the “protocol.”

    • REST. The modern, lightweight style — simple, fast, the default for most connections today.
    • SOAP. An older, more formal style still common in established enterprise systems.
    • API. The published “menu” of requests a system agrees to accept.

    Key: REST and SOAP are just two dialects for the same conversation — systems exchanging requests and responses so data can flow between them.

    ETLIntegrationHub ETL & Data Import79
    BUILD & AUTOMATION

    ETL is how messy data from outside gets cleaned up and loaded into the platform in the right place — without manual copy-paste.

    ETL stands for Extract, Transform, Load: pull data in, reshape it to match how your system expects it, then save it into the correct tables.

    EXTRACT
    Pull the raw data in — a spreadsheet, an export, another system.
    TRANSFORM
    Map columns, fix formats, and match it to your structure.

    IntegrationHub ETL gives you a guided, visual way to define those mapping rules so imports are consistent and repeatable — the same source loads correctly every time.

    Key: ETL turns a pile of outside data into clean, correctly-filed records the platform can actually use.

    NOW ASSISTNow Assist: Generative AI on the Platform80
    AI ON THE PLATFORM

    Now Assist is ServiceNow’s generative AI helper — it drafts, summarizes, and suggests, right inside the tools people already use.

    Generative AI can read and write natural language. Now Assist puts that to work on everyday tasks: summarizing a long case so an agent gets the gist instantly, drafting a reply, or even helping a builder generate part of a flow from a plain description.

    • Summarize. Condense long records, chats, or histories into a quick recap.
    • Draft. Generate first-pass replies, resolution notes, or knowledge articles.
    • Assist building. Help create flows and code from plain-language prompts.

    Key: Now Assist saves time on reading and writing tasks — people review and approve, but start from a draft instead of a blank page.

    AI & analytics

    PREDICTIVE AIPredictive Intelligence: Machine Learning at Work81
    AI ON THE PLATFORM

    Predictive Intelligence learns from your past records to make smart guesses about new ones — like which team a request should go to.

    Instead of following rigid rules, it studies thousands of resolved cases and spots patterns. When a new item arrives, it can suggest the right category, route it to the correct group, or flag similar past cases — automatically.

    CLASSIFY
    Predict the right category or priority for a new record.
    ROUTE
    Send work to the team most likely to resolve it.

    It improves as it sees more examples, so accuracy grows over time — cutting the manual triage that slows teams down.

    Key: Predictive Intelligence learns from your own history to sort, route, and prioritize work without someone deciding each time.

    VIRTUAL AGENTVirtual Agent & Conversational Interfaces82
    AI ON THE PLATFORM

    Virtual Agent is a chatbot that handles common requests through conversation — so people get answers without waiting for a human.

    Users type (or speak) what they need in plain language — “reset my password,” “order a laptop” — and the Virtual Agent walks them through it, taking real action behind the scenes. When something is too complex, it hands off smoothly to a person.

    • Topic. A guided conversation for one task, built like a flow.
    • Handoff. Passing the chat to a human when needed — with full context.
    • Channels. Works in chat windows, messaging apps, and portals.

    Key: Virtual Agent deflects routine requests through natural conversation, freeing people for the work that truly needs them.

    AI SEARCHAI Search & the Knowledge Experience83
    AI ON THE PLATFORM

    AI Search helps people find the right answer fast — understanding what they mean, not just matching exact words.

    Traditional search needs the perfect keyword. AI Search understands intent and context, so a question phrased in everyday language still surfaces the relevant knowledge article, catalog item, or record — often with the answer highlighted directly.

    UNDERSTANDS
    Reads the meaning behind a question, not just the literal words.
    SURFACES
    Ranks the most relevant results and can show the answer inline.

    Paired with a solid knowledge base, it means employees and customers help themselves — fewer tickets, faster resolutions.

    Key: AI Search turns a search box into an answer engine — people find what they need without knowing the exact words.

    ANALYTICSPerformance Analytics, Reporting & Dashboards84
    AI ON THE PLATFORM

    These tools turn the data flowing through the platform into clear pictures — so leaders can see how things are going and where to improve.

    A report is a snapshot answering one question right now (“how many open requests?”). A dashboard gathers several views on one screen. Performance Analytics adds the dimension of time — tracking trends so you see whether things are getting better or worse.

    NOW
    reports snapshot
    ONE VIEW
    dashboards combine
    OVER TIME
    analytics trend
    • KPI. A key number you watch, like average resolution time.
    • Indicator. A measure tracked day over day to reveal direction.

    Key: Reports show what’s happening, dashboards bring it together, and Performance Analytics shows whether it’s trending the right way.

    SPMStrategic Portfolio Management85
    BUSINESS VALUE

    Once known as ITBM, Strategic Portfolio Management (SPM) is where leaders connect big-picture strategy to the day-to-day work that delivers it.

    Most organizations have plenty of ideas and not enough hands. SPM is the layer that helps decide what to work on, fund it, staff it, and track whether it actually paid off — all in one place instead of scattered spreadsheets.

    STRATEGY
    Set goals and outcomes, then link every initiative back to them.
    PLANNING
    Build roadmaps and align budgets to priorities.
    DELIVERY
    Run the work as projects or agile, and watch progress live.
    VALUE
    Measure results against the goals you started with.

    Key: SPM answers the questions executives actually ask — are we working on the right things, and are they worth it?

    PLANNINGProject, Demand & Resource Management86
    BUSINESS VALUE

    Three connected disciplines that turn a flood of requests into an organized, staffed plan of work.

    • Demand Management. A single front door for new ideas and requests. Each one is captured, scored, and reviewed so good ideas rise and weak ones are filtered early.
    • Project Management. Once a demand is approved, it becomes a project with tasks, timelines, milestones, and a budget — tracked the same way every time.
    • Resource Management. Matches the people you have to the work you committed to, so teams aren’t overbooked or sitting idle.

    Because demand, projects, and resources live together, a leader can see — in real time — what’s requested, what’s approved, who’s assigned, and what’s at risk.

    BUSINESS VALUE

    Rather than buying software outright, you license the right to use specific applications for a period of time. Pricing generally follows a few common patterns.

    Products are bundled into tiers — often labeled Standard, Professional, and Enterprise — where higher tiers unlock more automation, AI, and advanced features.

    PERSONASThe People Who Use ServiceNow88

    • Fulfiller. The staff member who does the work behind a request — an IT agent, an HR specialist, a facilities tech.
